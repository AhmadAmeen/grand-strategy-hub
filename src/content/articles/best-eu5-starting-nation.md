Picking your first nation in EU5 is a big decision. Pick someone too weak and you'll be crushed. Pick someone too complex and you'll be overwhelmed. The sweet spot is a nation that's strong enough to survive your mistakes but simple enough to learn the game. Here are the best starting nations for new EU5 players.

## The Tutorial Pick: Portugal

Portugal is the classic beginner nation, and it's still a great choice in EU5. Here's why:

- **Safe position.** Portugal is on the edge of Europe, protected by Spain and the ocean. No one can easily invade you.
- **Clear goals.** Portugal's path is obvious — expand overseas, build a colonial empire, and dominate trade.
- **Strong economy.** Portugal has access to good trade routes and can build a strong trade economy early.
- **Simple management.** Portugal's internal politics are relatively simple compared to larger nations.

The classic Portugal run: build your economy, colonize the New World, and become a trade superpower. It's the perfect first campaign.

## The Strong Pick: France

France is the strongest nation in Europe at the start of EU5, and it's a great pick for beginners who want power:

- **Massive army.** France has one of the largest armies in the game. You can win most early wars.
- **Rich economy.** France has high development and a strong tax base.
- **Room to expand.** France can expand in every direction — into Italy, the Low Countries, or the Holy Roman Empire.

The downside is that France is a major power, which means everyone watches you. You'll face coalitions and diplomatic pressure. But for a beginner, having a strong army to fall back on is a huge safety net.

## The Relaxed Pick: Castile/Spain

Castile is the other Iberian power, and it's a great pick for a more relaxed campaign:

- **Strong position.** Castile is powerful in Iberia and has a clear path to forming Spain.
- **Colonial opportunities.** Like Portugal, Castile can expand into the New World.
- **Manageable challenges.** The Iberian region is relatively stable, and you can expand at your own pace.

The downside is that you'll eventually need to deal with France to your north. But that's a problem for later.

## The Powerful Pick: The Ottomans

The Ottomans are the strongest nation in the game, and they're a great pick for beginners who want to feel powerful:

- **Massive army and economy.** The Ottomans start with a huge military and a strong economy.
- **Expansion in every direction.** You can expand into Europe, the Middle East, or North Africa.
- **Strong military traditions.** The Ottoman military is one of the best in the game.

The downside is that managing a large empire is more complex. You have more provinces, more pops, and more to keep track of. It's a good second or third campaign, but maybe not the first.

## The Trade Pick: Venice

Venice is a small but wealthy nation, and it's a great pick for beginners who want to focus on trade:

- **Trade powerhouse.** Venice is one of the richest trade nations in the game.
- **Small and manageable.** Venice is a small nation, which means less to manage.
- **Clear focus.** Venice's path is clear — dominate trade in the Mediterranean.

The downside is that Venice is small and surrounded by larger powers. You'll need to be careful with your diplomacy.

## What to Look For in a First Nation

If you don't want to pick one of these specifically, here's what to look for:

- **A safe position.** You want to be able to make mistakes without being instantly crushed.
- **A clear path.** Whether it's colonization, trade, or conquest, a clear goal gives you direction.
- **A manageable size.** A medium-sized nation is easier to manage than a massive empire.
- **A strong economy.** A good economy gives you room to make mistakes.

## The Bottom Line

For your first EU5 campaign, pick **Portugal** — it's safe, has a clear path, and teaches you the game without punishing you too harshly. Once you've done that, try **France** for a more powerful campaign, or **Venice** for a trade-focused one.

The key is to pick a nation that gives you room to learn. You'll have plenty of time to play the hard starts once you know what you're doing.
