Starting as a single county in CK3 is the classic underdog experience. You're weak, your neighbors are stronger, and your liege (if you have one) might decide to revoke your title at any moment. But it's also one of the most rewarding ways to play — building a dynasty from a single county to an empire is the heart of CK3. Here's how to survive the early game.

## Pick the Right Start

Not all single-county starts are created equal. Some are genuinely brutal, others are surprisingly comfortable. When you're picking your start:

- **Look for a county with good development.** High development means more taxes and levies. The counties in Italy, France, and the Byzantine Empire are generally strong.
- **Check your liege.** If you start as a vassal, your liege matters a lot. A weak liege means you can expand within the realm without much interference. A strong liege means protection but also more oversight.
- **Consider your neighbors.** You want neighbors you can beat, or at least neighbors who won't immediately attack you. A county surrounded by stronger counts is a death sentence.

## Manage Your Liege

If you start as a vassal, your relationship with your liege is your lifeline. Here's how to manage it:

- **Send gifts.** A small gift goes a long way toward keeping your liege happy. It's worth the gold.
- **Join their wars.** Even if you don't contribute much, joining your liege's wars gives you a relationship boost and shows loyalty.
- **Don't scheme against them early.** You might be tempted to join a faction to install a new liege, but early on, you're too weak to benefit. Focus on building your own power first.
- **Watch your obligations.** If your liege increases your tax or levy obligations, it hurts. Keep your relationship high to discourage this.

## Build Your Economy

Your economy is your foundation. A single county doesn't generate much, so you need to make every coin count:

- **Build economic buildings first.** The farmlands and tradeport buildings are your bread and butter. Prioritize buildings that boost tax income.
- **Develop your capital.** Spending gold on development is a long-term investment, but it pays off. A developed capital means more taxes, more levies, and more men-at-arms.
- **Don't overspend on men-at-arms.** You need some military, but don't bankrupt yourself. A small, well-equipped force is better than a large, poorly-funded one.
- **Marry for money.** Marrying a rich spouse can give you a significant income boost. Look for spouses with high stewardship.

## Expand Carefully

Expansion as a small county is a delicate balance. You need to grow, but you can't afford to overextend:

- **Fabricate claims.** Your chancellor can fabricate claims on neighboring counties. This is your primary expansion tool early on. It takes time, but it's reliable.
- **Marry for claims.** Marrying someone with a claim on a neighboring county gives you a way to press that claim. This is a powerful expansion method.
- **Attack weak neighbors.** Look for neighbors who are at war, have low troop counts, or are dealing with internal revolts. That's your window.
- **Don't take on too much.** Conquering one county at a time is fine. Conquering three at once will stretch your resources and make your neighbors nervous.

## Build Alliances

Alliances are your shield. A strong alliance can deter attackers and give you the military support you need to expand:

- **Marry your children into powerful families.** This is the most reliable way to build alliances. Marry your heir to a powerful duke's daughter, and you have a powerful ally.
- **Marry yourself well.** If you're unmarried, a good marriage can bring an alliance immediately.
- **Use your alliances.** Don't just sit on them — call your allies into your wars. They'll do the heavy lifting while you take the territory.

## Survive the First Crisis

Every small county campaign hits a crisis — a war you can't win, a liege who wants your title, a faction that wants you gone. Here's how to survive:

- **Surrender if you have to.** Losing a war isn't the end. You lose some territory or gold, but you live to fight another day. A dead ruler is a game over.
- **Buy time.** If you're being attacked, raise your levies, hire mercenaries if you can afford them, and call your allies. Even a losing battle can buy you time for reinforcements.
- **Go into debt if you must.** Debt is recoverable. A lost title is not. Don't be afraid to take loans to survive.

## The Bottom Line

Surviving as a small county is about patience and smart management. Build your economy, manage your liege, expand carefully, and build alliances. Don't try to do everything at once — focus on surviving the first few decades, and the growth will come.

The early game as a small county is the hardest part of CK3, but it's also the most rewarding. Stick with it, and you'll be amazed at what a single county can become.
