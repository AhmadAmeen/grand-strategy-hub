Estates are one of the most important internal systems in EU5 — and one of the most different from EU4. In EU4, estates were a side system you interacted with occasionally. In EU5, they're central to how you manage your realm. Here's what you need to know.

## What Are Estates?

Estates are the powerful social groups within your realm — the nobility, clergy, burghers, and other classes who hold power and influence. They have their own interests, their own demands, and their own ways of helping or hindering you.

## Why Estates Matter

Estates aren't optional — you can't ignore them. They control significant portions of your realm's economy and military. How you manage them determines whether your realm is stable and prosperous, or beset by rebellion and infighting.

**What estates provide:**
- **Nobility** — military power, generals, and manpower
- **Clergy** — stability, conversion, and legitimacy
- **Burghers** — trade income, tax revenue, and economic growth

## Estate Influence

Each estate has an **influence** level — how much power they wield in your realm. Influence is affected by:

- **Privileges** — granting an estate privileges increases their influence
- **Actions** — some actions increase or decrease estate influence
- **Events** — events can shift the balance of power between estates

**High influence** means the estate can demand more from you and is harder to control. **Low influence** means they're weaker but also less helpful.

## Estate Loyalty/Opinion

Estates also have a **loyalty** or opinion level — how happy they are with your rule. Loyalty is affected by:

- **Privileges** — granting privileges makes an estate more loyal
- **Actions** — fulfilling their demands increases loyalty; ignoring them decreases it
- **Events** — events can change estate loyalty

**High loyalty** means the estate provides their bonuses at full strength. **Low loyalty** means they withhold their support — or worse, actively work against you.

## Managing Estates Effectively

**1. Don't let one estate dominate.** A nobility with too much influence can challenge your authority. Keep estates balanced.

**2. Grant privileges strategically.** Privileges give estates more power in exchange for bonuses. They're useful, but they're hard to revoke. Don't grant privileges you'll regret.

**3. Complete estate agendas.** Estates have agendas — tasks they want you to complete. Completing them improves loyalty and gives you bonuses.

**4. Watch the balance of power.** If one estate becomes too powerful, take actions to reduce their influence — even if it makes them unhappy in the short term.

## Common Mistakes

- **Ignoring estates entirely.** Ignored estates become disloyal, which means lost bonuses and potential rebellion.
- **Granting too many privileges.** Privileges are a slippery slope — each one makes the estate harder to control.
- **Letting one estate dominate.** A single dominant estate is a threat to your authority.
- **Not completing agendas.** Agendas are easy bonuses. Ignoring them wastes free value.

## The Bottom Line

Estates are the internal politics of EU5. They're not a side system — they're central to how your realm functions. Manage them well, and they'll be your strongest supporters. Manage them poorly, and they'll be the greatest threat to your rule.

The key is **balance**: keep your estates happy enough to provide their bonuses, but not so powerful that they can challenge your authority. It's a delicate dance — but mastering it is essential to becoming a great EU5 ruler.
