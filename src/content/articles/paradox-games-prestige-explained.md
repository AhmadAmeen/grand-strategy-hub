Prestige is one of those words Paradox loves. It shows up in EU4, EU5, and CK3, and it means something slightly different in each game. If you're coming from one Paradox game to another, the differences can be confusing. Here's how prestige (and its cousin, renown) works across the three big grand strategy titles.

## Prestige in EU4

In EU4, prestige is a **national resource** — it belongs to your nation, not your ruler. It's a number from -100 to +100 that affects:

- **Diplomatic reputation** — higher prestige makes other nations like you more
- **Trade power** — higher prestige gives you a trade bonus
- **Military morale** — higher prestige gives your armies a small morale boost
- **Monthly gain** — you gain prestige over time based on your actions

You earn prestige by winning wars, completing missions, and making good decisions. You lose it by losing wars, breaking truces, and making bad decisions.

**The key difference from CK3:** EU4 prestige is a **national** stat. It's not tied to a character — it's tied to the nation itself. It's also a **modifier**, not a currency. You don't spend it; it just makes you better or worse at various things.

## Prestige in EU5

EU5's prestige system is still being refined, but it follows EU4's model — it's a national resource that affects your diplomatic standing and various bonuses. The exact mechanics are still being tuned as the game gets patched and expanded.

**The key takeaway:** If you're coming from EU4, EU5's prestige will feel familiar. It's a national stat that reflects your nation's reputation and affects your interactions with other nations.

## Prestige in CK3

CK3's prestige is completely different. It's a **personal resource** — it belongs to your current ruler, not your dynasty or your realm. It's a currency you spend on:

- **Men-at-arms** — hiring and maintaining elite troops
- **Casus bellis** — declaring wars
- **Decisions** — many decisions have a prestige cost
- **Court actions** — some interactions cost prestige

You earn prestige by winning wars, holding events, and being a successful ruler. When your ruler dies, their prestige is gone — your heir starts fresh.

**The key difference from EU4:** CK3 prestige is a **personal currency**. It's tied to your character, it's spent on actions, and it resets on succession. It's a much more active resource than EU4's passive modifier.

## Renown in CK3

Renown is CK3's **dynasty resource**. It's separate from prestige and it's the long-term resource that makes your dynasty stronger over generations. You earn it from your dynasty's achievements — members holding titles, winning wars, living long lives. You spend it on:

- **Legacy perks** — permanent bonuses for your entire dynasty
- **Disinheriting** — removing children from the succession line

**The key takeaway:** Renown is the resource that compounds over time. It's the difference between a dynasty that grows stronger with each generation and one that resets every succession.

## The Big Picture

Here's the simplest way to think about it:

- **EU4 prestige** = a national reputation stat. Passive, affects your standing.
- **EU5 prestige** = similar to EU4, still being refined.
- **CK3 prestige** = a personal currency. Active, spent on actions, resets on succession.
- **CK3 renown** = a dynasty resource. Long-term, invested in permanent bonuses.

The common thread is that all of these systems reward success. Win wars, make good decisions, and your reputation grows. Fail, and it shrinks. The difference is in how that reputation is used — as a passive modifier in EU4, or as an active currency in CK3.

## The Bottom Line

If you're moving between Paradox games, the biggest adjustment is understanding that prestige means different things in different games. In EU4, it's a national stat you don't think about much. In CK3, it's a personal currency you're constantly spending. And renown is the long-term resource that makes your dynasty stronger over generations.

Once you understand which system you're dealing with, you'll know how to use it. And that's the key to mastering any Paradox game.
