## What Are Trade Nodes?

Every province in EU4 feeds its trade value into a **trade node**. Trade nodes are the hubs of the global economy. They collect value from the provinces around them, and that value either gets collected by a nation with trade power in the node, or it flows onward to the next node.

Think of it like a river system. Provinces are the streams that feed into the river (the trade node). The river flows downstream, and at each junction, the water splits based on how much control each branch has. Some of the water gets diverted (collected by nations), and the rest keeps flowing toward the sea.

## The Three End Nodes

The trade network has three **end nodes**, the "seas" where trade value finally settles:

- **English Channel:** the richest end node in the game, fed by trade from the New World, Africa, and Northern Europe
- **Genoa:** the Mediterranean end node, fed by trade from the Middle East, North Africa, and the Atlantic
- **Venice:** the eastern Mediterranean end node, fed by trade from the Levant, the Balkans, and beyond

Once value reaches an end node, it stays there. It doesn't flow anywhere else. It just sits, waiting for a nation to collect it. This is why controlling an end node is so powerful: you're at the end of the line, and everyone's trade has to pass through you.

## How Value Flows

Here's the key mechanic that confuses most new players: **trade value doesn't pick one path. It splits across every available route.**

If a node has three outgoing connections, the value flowing out of that node splits across all three, proportionally to the trade power each downstream node has. If you control 50% of the trade power in one downstream node, you capture 50% of the value that flows there.

This means:
- **Trade power matters everywhere**, not just in your home node
- **Controlling the path** is often as important as controlling the source
- **Merchants** can steer trade toward your preferred route, but they can't stop the split entirely

## Why This Matters for Your Campaign

Understanding the trade network changes how you play:

**1. Your home node is your foundation.** The node where your capital sits is where you collect trade. The more trade power you have there, the more of the value you capture.

**2. Expand toward the end nodes.** The closer you are to an end node, the more value flows through your territory. A nation in the English Channel collects far more than a nation in the middle of the network.

**3. Trade power compounds.** The more provinces you control in a node, the more trade power you have, which means you capture more value, which means you can afford more provinces. It's a snowball effect.

**4. Don't ignore upstream.** Even if your home node is downstream, controlling key upstream nodes lets you steer more value toward yourself.
